01 / About

How I found cybersecurity

Before I knew terms like SIEM or DFIR, I was already drawn to investigations through true-crime documentaries, podcasts and forensic stories.

What held my attention was never just the crime. It was the process: how one overlooked detail could change the timeline, how investigators tested different explanations, and how evidence separated what probably happened from what could actually be proved.

That curiosity led me to cybersecurity and, eventually, to SOC and defensive security work. I enjoy following activity across logs, network traffic and host evidence, then explaining clearly what the evidence does and does not show.

I came into tech from a background in languages and administration. It taught me how to research carefully, organise information and communicate clearly—skills I now bring into technical investigations and reporting.

02 / How I work

My approach

I am still building depth, but these are the habits I bring to practical security work.

01

Start with the evidence

I work from the available logs, packets and artefacts before deciding what the activity means.

02

Test the conclusion

I compare suspicious activity with benign behaviour and check whether missing telemetry could change the finding.

03

Keep the work clear

I document the steps, limitations and evidence so another person can understand and review the result.

04

Learn by building

I use projects and structured training to turn new concepts into practical work I can explain and defend.
