01 / About

Curiosity became a method.

My route into cybersecurity began with a long-standing interest in investigations and forensics: what happened, what evidence supports it, and what other explanation might still fit.

That instinct now shapes how I approach defensive security. I do not treat an alert as a conclusion. I trace it back to source evidence, test the logic around it and document what is known, inferred and still uncertain.

I moved into cybersecurity from a language and administrative background, bringing clear writing, structured thinking and the ability to explain technical work without hiding behind jargon. My current focus is building practical depth across SOC analysis, detection engineering, incident response and network security.

02 / What I bring

Work that holds up beyond the first answer.

I combine investigation, engineering and communication so that findings are useful to both technical teams and decision-makers.

01

Investigate ambiguity

Build timelines, validate indicators and separate observed facts from assumptions when the evidence is incomplete or noisy.

02

Engineer detections

Translate adversary behaviour into portable rules, then test those rules against variation, benign activity and telemetry failure.

03

Validate controls

Use positive, negative and return-path testing to confirm that network and detection controls behave as designed.

04

Communicate findings

Connect technical evidence to risk, containment and next actions without overstating what the data can prove.
